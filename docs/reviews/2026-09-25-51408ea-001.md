### SEC-18-001: robots.txt is read without a size cap or deadline

- Severity: LOW
- Disposition: RECOMMENDATION
- Category: resource-abuse
- Where: src/collegium/acquisition/fetching.py:116-126 (`_load_robots`), called from `fetch` at line 72 on every hop to a new site
- Status: new

`_load_robots` uses `self._client.get(...)`, which reads and decompresses the whole response. It does not go through the streaming loop, so neither `max_bytes` nor `give_up_at` applies.

- **Time:** a site that sends one byte of robots.txt every 19 s holds the fetch indefinitely, because each read stays within httpx's 20 s timeout. The worker has no job-level timeout (no lease or timeout in worker.py), so one such site stalls the single worker, and with it the whole organization.
- **Memory:** a gzip-encoded robots.txt expands about 1000:1 with no cap, so a modest transfer can exhaust the worker's memory. The job is then retried and does the same again.

Any address the organization reads can trigger this: a search result, a feed item, or a redirect target, because robots.txt is loaded for each new site at each hop. The fetch is keyed to the site, not the page, so a hostile site can target Collegium by its User-Agent. This is not new code. It does contradict the new docstring ("however slowly the site sends, is given up") and the decisions.md entry ("redirects included").

**Fix:** Fetch robots.txt through the same bounded streaming loop, with a small cap (for example 500 KB, Google's limit) and the remaining deadline of the calling fetch. Treat a response that is too large or too slow as "no rules", or as a refusal.

### SEC-19-001: The deadline is checked only between reads, so handshake and headers can trickle past it

- Severity: LOW
- Disposition: RECOMMENDATION
- Category: resource-abuse
- Where: src/collegium/acquisition/fetching.py:70-87
- Status: new

You asked whether one blocking read can exceed the deadline. It can, by at most one read timeout: 20 s for pages, 30 s for `FeedReader`. That part is acceptable. The larger overshoot is elsewhere. After the check at the top of a hop, `self._client.stream(...)` does DNS, connect, the TLS handshake, the request write and the reading of the response headers before any further check. Each of these obeys only the per-operation timeout:

- A server can trickle the TLS handshake or the headers byte by byte. h11 accepts about 16 KB of incomplete headers, and each read may take just under 20 s, so a single response can stay open for hours.
- `_check` calls `socket.getaddrinfo`, which has no timeout of its own.
- If the last check passes just before the deadline, a normal but slow hop still adds a connect, a handshake and a headers read, about 40-60 s.

Without a trickling server, the real worst case is roughly the deadline plus 60 s, which is acceptable. With a deliberately trickling server, the time is not bounded.

**Fix:** Enforce the deadline from outside the blocking calls. Options:
- A `threading.Timer` that closes the response or client at `give_up_at`, which aborts the blocked read.
- Running the fetch through `httpx.AsyncClient` under `asyncio.timeout`.
- A job-level wall-clock limit in the worker, which would also cover SEC-18 and other providers.

Keep the chunk check, since it gives the clean error.

### SEC-20-001: `find_feed` tries every announced feed link, each now with 20 MB and 120 s

- Severity: LOW
- Disposition: RECOMMENDATION
- Category: resource-abuse
- Where: src/collegium/acquisition/web.py:97-111
- Status: new

The candidates are every `<link type="application/(rss|atom)+xml" href=...>` in the first 200 KB of the homepage, plus the 6 `FEED_PATHS`. They are de-duplicated, but not limited in number. `urljoin` lets the hrefs point at any other host.

A hostile homepage can list thousands of links. Each is now allowed 20 MB of download, a 20 MB `bytearray` plus a `bytes` copy, a defusedxml parse, and 120 s (plus the SEC-19 overshoot). One strategize job could therefore spend hours and many GB of traffic. It could also make the organization request thousands of third-party URLs in the operator's name (the User-Agent carries `COLLEGIUM_CONTACT`).

Before this commit each candidate had the 3 MB cap, and no time bound at all, so time exposure per link is actually better now. The count, not the per-link cap, is what makes the larger cap matter. The exposure is limited:
- Only the Strategist calls `find_feed` (strategist.py:272).
- It checks at most `MAX_SOURCE_PROPOSALS = 2` sites per job.
- A site is a candidate only after it has been used at least twice in the domain within 30 days, and social media and forums are never proposed.

Memory per candidate is bounded: about 40 MB for the body plus the ElementTree for a 20 MB document, one candidate at a time.

**Fix:** Try at most a few announced links (for example 3), preferably on the same site, before `FEED_PATHS`. Or give `find_feed` one deadline for all its candidates, which it could pass as the remaining time to each `fetch`.
